Plan a Safer Day on the Water

Updated July 2026
Practical coastal travel guidance

A good beach day starts before anyone enters the water. Check the local forecast, posted warnings, surf conditions, and lifeguard information when you arrive. The National Park Service recommends swimming with a buddy, supervising children closely, using sun protection, and staying out of the water during thunderstorms. Conditions can change quickly, so plans should change with them.

Rip currents can pull swimmers away from shore. If that happens, stay calm and do not fight the current. National Park Service guidance recommends swimming parallel to the shoreline until you move beyond the current, then working back toward shore. If you cannot escape, float or tread water and signal for help. Always follow the instructions posted at the specific beach you are visiting.

Prepare Before You Paddle

Choose a route that matches the least experienced paddler in the group. Wear an appropriate life jacket, inspect the kayak and paddle, secure drinking water and sun protection, and keep a reliable way to call for help. Tell someone on shore where you are going and when you expect to return. Guided trips are a practical choice when the coastline, tides, or local weather patterns are unfamiliar.

Explore Reefs Responsibly

Coral reefs are living ecosystems, not scenery to touch or collect. NOAA recommends a reef-friendly approach to sun protection, including protective clothing that can reduce the amount of sunscreen needed. While snorkeling or diving, maintain control of fins and equipment, never stand on coral, give wildlife space, and follow all local sanctuary or protected-area rules.

Sources: National Park Service beach and water safety guidance, National Park Service swimming safety guidance, and NOAA Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary reef guidance.
